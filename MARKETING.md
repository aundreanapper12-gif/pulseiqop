# PulseIQ Customer Acquisition Playbook

## Core positioning

PulseIQ is not sold as “AI analytics software.” The customer-facing problem is simpler:

> **Stop wondering where the money went. Make the numbers point to it.**

The free product helps an owner quantify where a financial or operating signal deserves investigation. The paid offer goes into the supporting data to test the likely cause and recommend what to do next.

### One-sentence pitch

PulseIQ helps growing service businesses turn revenue, labor, expense, lead, and rework data into a ranked list of where money may be leaking, what to investigate first, and what recovering part of the opportunity could mean financially.

### What PulseIQ should never claim

Do not promise guaranteed savings, guaranteed recovered revenue, guaranteed profit, or that a target variance proves waste. Do not call a modeled opportunity “lost revenue” without the qualifying assumptions. The strongest marketing advantage is credibility: PulseIQ labels direct arithmetic separately from modeled opportunity.

## Ideal early customer

The best early prospect is an owner or operator of a service business who already has business activity and reports but does not have a dedicated analyst translating them into decisions. Strong signals include recurring payroll or overtime questions, missed calls or leads, refund/credit problems, repeat work, software creep, inconsistent location performance, or a general feeling that revenue is healthy but margin is harder to explain.

Avoid trying to be “analytics for every company” at launch. Early messaging should stay focused on service businesses where labor, customer demand, scheduling, lead capture, repeat work, and recurring operating costs are visible enough to analyze.

## Offer ladder

### Free — PulseIQ Snapshot

Purpose: generate trust and reveal a financially meaningful problem.

The user enters monthly actuals and targets, optionally completes missed-lead and rework models, receives ranked findings, sees a recovery scenario, and gets a Fix This First plan.

Primary conversion question: **“Is this signal large enough that I need to know why it is happening?”**

### $149 — Quick Leak Check

Purpose: low-friction first paid engagement.

Use when the customer has one clear expensive question and one focused dataset. The deliverable should answer the question, surface the most useful patterns, and give three prioritized recommendations.

### $399 — Profit Leak Analysis

Purpose: flagship analysis.

Use when the customer needs multiple data sources connected to the financial signal. The report should show the size of the issue, segmentation/root-cause evidence, assumptions and limitations, prioritized actions, and the metrics that should confirm improvement.

### $199/month — Monthly Pulse

Purpose: recurring revenue after value has been demonstrated.

Do not lead with the subscription for a brand-new prospect. Offer it after an initial analysis when the business has recurring metrics worth monitoring and wants ongoing prioritization.

## Primary funnel

**Problem-aware visitor → homepage → free workspace → quantified finding → request deeper analysis → checkout → delivery → Monthly Pulse follow-up**

Every marketing channel should point to the free workspace unless the prospect already has a specific paid analysis need. The free diagnostic is more persuasive than asking a cold lead to immediately buy consulting.

## Demo story

Use the fictional BrightPath Home Services example consistently.

The narrative should be:

“BrightPath looks busy and revenue is substantial. PulseIQ finds that overtime and payroll are above the business’s own targets, then separates those direct variances from a modeled missed-lead opportunity. Instead of saying ‘cut labor,’ PulseIQ tells the owner to compare four weeks of overtime by employee and shift before changing staffing.”

The point of the demo is not the fictional dollar amount. The point is to show that PulseIQ moves from **number → question → first action → measurement**.

## Social content pillars

### 1. “Where did the money go?”

Short posts showing how a profitable-looking month can still hide payroll drift, overtime, refunds, subscriptions, missed demand, or repeat work.

### 2. One metric is not the answer

Explain why high payroll does not automatically mean overstaffing, why missed calls are not automatically lost revenue, and why refunds need reason-level analysis before action.

### 3. Dollar-first analysis

Show how to rank problems by financial impact instead of trying to improve every KPI at the same time.

### 4. Before you cut costs

Educational posts about finding root cause before cutting people, marketing, software, inventory, or service capacity.

### 5. Fix and prove

Show that every recommendation needs a measurement plan: what changed, what metric should improve, and what side effects must be watched.

## Sample social posts

### Post 1

Revenue can be up and the business can still feel broke.

That usually means the next question is not “How do we sell more?” It is “What changed underneath the revenue?”

Payroll. Overtime. Refunds. Missed leads. Repeat work. Software. Fulfillment.

PulseIQ is built to rank those signals in dollars so an owner knows which question is worth investigating first.

A $3,000 payroll variance does **not** automatically mean “cut hours.”

It means you have a $3,000 question.

Did demand move? Did schedules stop matching peak hours? Did overtime spike? Did a new location ramp? Did workload change?

Every educational page should link naturally to `/workspace` as the practical next step.

## Referral partners

Potential referral relationships include bookkeepers, accountants, fractional CFOs, operations consultants, marketing agencies, call-answering providers, payroll advisors, and business coaches whose clients may discover an operational question their existing service does not investigate. Keep the positioning complementary: PulseIQ helps identify and analyze operating signals; it does not replace licensed accounting, tax, or legal work.

## Sales discovery questions

Use these questions before recommending a paid package:

1. What number feels wrong or harder to explain right now?
2. When did you first notice it?
3. What changed around the same time—volume, staffing, schedule, pricing, location, vendor, channel, or process?
4. What data already exists that could explain the change?
5. What decision will you make differently if we find the cause?
6. How often does this problem recur?
7. What would a useful answer need to contain for you to act on it?

The package should follow the size of the question, not the other way around.

## Conversion measurement

Before paid advertising, track at minimum:

- Homepage → workspace click rate
- Workspace completion rate
- Percentage of scans with at least one completed finding
- Workspace → request-page click rate
- Request completion rate
- Checkout-start rate
- Successful purchase rate
- Quick Leak Check → Profit Leak Analysis upgrade rate
- One-time client → Monthly Pulse conversion rate
- Refund/cancellation rate
- Revenue per qualified lead

Do not optimize advertising around page views. Optimize around completed diagnostics, qualified analysis requests, and paid customers.

## Launch sequence

### Phase 1 — Proof

Use the free workspace with a small set of real business owners or operators. Watch where they get confused, which inputs they already know, which report language they trust, and which finding makes them want more detail. Do not scale traffic until checkout and delivery work end-to-end.

### Phase 2 — Founder-led acquisition

Use targeted direct outreach, professional networking, local business communities, referrals, and educational content. Send traffic to the free workspace. Record objections and improve the homepage, workspace labels, and request page based on repeated patterns.

### Phase 3 — Repeatable content and SEO

Publish problem-specific educational pages and demonstrations. Build around the operating questions prospects actually ask, not broad analytics terminology.

### Phase 4 — Paid acquisition

Only test paid traffic after the site can measure workspace completion → request → checkout → purchase. Start with one audience/problem combination at a time so the data tells you which message is actually producing customers.

## Launch rule

**Do not spend money to acquire traffic until the production deployment, business inbox, Stripe payment links, request flow, and at least one test-mode checkout have been verified end-to-end.**
